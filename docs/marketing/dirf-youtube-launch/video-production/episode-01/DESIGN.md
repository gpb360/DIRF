# Operational Precision — video system

DIRF's visual world is a calm control room surrounded by unresolved signals.
Noise and stale chat live at the edges. One cobalt route creates order through
the center. Green is never decorative: it appears only after evidence earns a
completed state. Amber always means a human decision or unresolved gate.

## Composition rules

- Anchor major type to the left or bottom edge; avoid centered web-card stacks.
- Use a background, a proof surface, and a foreground evidence layer in every
  frame.
- Headlines occupy 60–78% of the usable width in landscape and at least 80% in
  portrait.
- Use asymmetric zones, structural rules, registration marks, and restrained
  metadata to make each frame feel produced.
- Atmospheric images may establish the world, but all claims and labels remain
  native text for crisp rendering and easy revision.
- Ambient movement is deterministic: drift, pulse, camera push, or scan. No
  random particles and no infinite animation loops.

## Motion language

- Stale context drifts and fragments.
- Active routes draw, lock, and snap into place.
- Evidence stamps, checks, and resolves.
- Decisions hold in amber until a human acts.
- Scene changes use hard cuts for failure-mode shifts and measured wipes for
  DIRF model reveals.

## Avoid

- Template-like equal card grids.
- Neon cyberpunk decoration.
- Gradient-filled headline text.
- Confetti or celebratory completion theater.
- Tiny terminal text or UI-scale body copy.
- Claims of monitoring, certification, deployment, or authority DIRF does not
  have.
